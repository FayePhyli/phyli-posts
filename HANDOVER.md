## 4. Style preferences
**Overall:** colourful, sparkly, fun, vintage, but the editorial, high-end, pretty, refined version. Y2K meets editorial luxe. Maximal-ish but edited ("eclectic, sexy, sensual", collage-y, layered, purple and silver). Depth, never flat or 2D: real surfaces, layers, light, grain. Handmade beats perfect: imperfection is proof a human was there.

**Materials: always hers.** Her photos, her pastel washes, paintings and ink drawings, photographed from the paper and used **exactly as made**, never "perfected" or digitised (e.g. the PHYLI + lily collage screenshot). Never generated, never faked handwriting. **Updated 28 Oct:** for @faye.pressly collage and maximal pieces, images she saves into the Pinterest/reference folder CAN be cut out and collaged in; her own art is still the first choice.

**Fonts on @faye.pressly:** Rozha (display headline / reel captions), Newsreader (serif body) and Newsreader italic (asides, the little voice), IBM Plex Mono (small tracked labels), a bold grid-tile sans with glow (Inter Bold style) for covers.
- *Superseded for @faye.pressly:* Courier Prime typewriter reel captions and Plus Jakarta story-sans carousels (video-caption-style.md, 17–18 Oct). Bodoni Moda + DM Sans ("lame"), Arimo ("TikTok-y").

**Carousels:**
- Vary every one: layout, font size, colour, pacing, space, images. The same pastel background on every carousel is "too boring".
- Four approved layouts, used in rhythm: (1) black-and-white photo of her with the cover's pink bold type; (2) dark plum tile, big ivory serif, an aside in brackets; (3) cream editorial page, left-aligned; (4) her bio portrait faint behind the type with pink bold headline.
- Also approved: the **3D page** (her cut out, standing in front of the type, text behind her) used occasionally; the **tiger ink in silver on her pink painting** with writing across ("the womb" page); her pastel wash (lamp removed, lilac pushed, lifted) as a ground; inks filled with her photos or coloured scribbles; one highlighted line per page; a green dot as the end mark; the fairy ink once, bottom right, on a last slide.
- One or two ivory pages at most per carousel (more is "too phyli.studio").
- An ink drawing only where the page wants one, and it should suit the page.
- Humour lives in type placement: a small italic aside like a little voice.

**Reels:**
- Clips 1–3s. 2–3 cuts from one video are fine, **never next to each other**, nothing repetitive (no same selfie twice close together, no near-identical scenes, no slow-mo hair). A shot runs longer only when the words call for it (e.g. fire 5–6s, twice max; a selfie 4–5s, twice max, well apart).
- Long voice notes: cut to the best content, about two minutes tops.
- Captions appear word by word as she speaks, just below centre, no top hook text, no full stops at line ends.
- Favourite clip: the city walk in sunglasses and the leather bomber jacket (not the walking-away Palma street clip, which she finds depressing).
- Indigo can appear (face included) where she mentions Indigo; family footage only where she allows.
- Grade: she now prefers to apply **her own filter**. She grades, sends it back, Claude adds captions. App: CapCut for the edit (Tezza for her preset if that's where it lives).
- **Sound pulls against the image** (e.g. Marvin Gaye "Got to Give It Up" on the witchy Night Court post). Contrast, not match. Keep her voice loud and the track low on spoken pieces.

**Captions and copy:** see section 5 and the voice sheet.

## 5. Voice rules (short version; full in the voice sheet)
- Don't chop. "Rhysand was a great leader, so was Tamlin", never two clipped sentences for effect. A full stop at the end of a finished thought or paragraph is fine.
- On-screen lines and short captions are written the way she texts: lowercase, line breaks doing the work, no full stop at the end of a line.
- No hyphens or dashes as punctuation. Use "…", commas or nothing.
- A business is "she", never "it" ("launch her", not "launch it").
- Never "make it make sense", internet phrasing, process talk (one take, Sunday, cut down, swearing left in), "framework", "organisational design", "build/built" for what Phyli does (making, growing).
- Swearing: "f*king" on screen; at most one per piece; "I give no fucks / five percent" lives in the recording only, never captions.
- Humour is deadpan: the joke is the label that reframes something real ("Indigo's apartment" / "my sublet for 5 hours").
- Never take her words verbatim as the best version: elevate them, pull the threads, find the humour she doesn't foreground. But don't invent: distinguish lines she *explored* from lines she *chose*.
- Catch repeats and clunky lines yourself ("You should catch things like this").
- Send scripts in the chat itself, never as text files. No delivery coaching.

## 8. Social media best practice she's saved
(From the reels and carousels she asked to remember, Oct 2026.)

**Hooks**
- nedwagner's 10 that always work: Nobody mentions this · I wish I knew this earlier · Pause for a second · Ever notice this pattern? · Here's a real truth · Let me save you some time · This is going to surprise you · You need this now · You're not going to agree with this · I just figured this out.
- Madison: "Most people don't realise ___" as on-screen text over any B-roll; finish it with something the audience actually wants to know. A hook must stop the scroll **and** give a reason to stay: open an information gap ("there's one thing that changed…", "there's one mistake I see… that costs them…"), give enough to care, not enough to leave. Retention is king.
- Madison's title hooks: Don't know what to post? · If you're seeing this video, it's for a reason · Your life can change faster than you think · You have 93 days left · A do-er will beat an overthinker · Only future millionaires will see this video · Let me guess · What if God picked you?
- A credential ("I ran companies for twenty years") is not a hook.
- Comment-keyword CTA: "comment TASTE and I'll send you the playbook".

**Selling (Dawn's 7 rules)**: people buy with emotion, then justify with logic · they move away from pain faster than towards pleasure, so describe their pain better than they can · sales is clarity, not convincing ("this is for me") · most objections aren't the real one ("too expensive" often means "will it work for me?"), so make them feel safer · people act when staying the same feels worse ("if nothing changes in six months…") · detachment sells, need repels; keep showing up with the offer · most price objections are value objections.

**Targeting (Natalie, bossbabe)**: posts pop when written to one specific woman ("a massive overachiever who…"); be mega specific, a real person in a real situation; say it directly ("if you can relate, you are my ideal client"). In a world of AI, mega-specific real-person content wins.

**Formats and styling seen working**
- Series and recognisable recurring sets/outfits make a grid read as a series.
- Carousel hook: "If I were you, this is how I'd grow my account before the year ends:" with a small italic subline.
- One to three words on screen at a time, mid-frame; a clean sans with an occasional italic serif accent word.
- Story styles: huge rounded bold text in one colour over a quiet image; a quote set inside an object in the photo; raw-honesty text over an unpolished selfie; one key word in a second colour.
- Matt Gray: org-chart carousel ("7 AI employees") on a moody photo with one accent-coloured headline word.

**Trends she's tracking**
- Julia Broome: early-2000s "someone's diary, not everyone's storefront", the glitter era, retiring beige, the pen-to-paper renaissance, longer-form content returning.
- Gen Z / new luxury (Anastasia Shtompel): taste has gone flat; status is what you understand, not what you can afford; imperfection is proof someone was there; privacy is the new visibility; build a world, reward curiosity, think smaller, show the human hand, create rituals, protect some access. Full notes in the brand and market notes doc.

**What lands for her specifically**: her authenticity, heart and voice ALWAYS get a big response. Her humour and weirdness rarely get shown, so put them in. Mid-motion and imperfect beats posed (aerial hoop 4.1K, dance mirror 3.2K; static portraits ~700–1,600). Plain text carousels in her voice did well ("I am… / I love… / I'm still working on…", 137 likes, 35 comments). She's comfortable with voiceover over B-roll, dancing, painting and handwriting; talking to camera only rarely, when the point is very clear. She speaks in long sentences, pauses and laughs; scripts should flow and sound elegant like the book but human, with tension, edge or surprise in each.
