VOICE · seven voiceovers (about 9 minutes total)

1 · THE REINTRODUCTION (post 1, your first reel · ~60s)
I ran companies for close to twenty years. The thing nobody told me, and the thing I never once found in a business book, is that a company behaves far more like a body than like a machine. It has a skeleton. It has a nervous system. It braces. It heals badly. It gets tired in ways no spreadsheet will ever show you. So I wrote the book I needed. Twelve volumes, and each one is a part of a company you have felt but never had a name for. I'm going to read from it here, one volume at a time, starting with the one your November needs most. If you run a product business that works beautifully on paper and still feels heavier every month, stay. This is for you.

2 · CARRYING IT (post 2 · ~65s)
Here's something I see in almost every product brand I read. The physical operation is good. Materials become product, product gets packed, it ships, it arrives. She learned that the hard way, probably in a spare room before it was ever a studio. And she's exhausted. Because there is a second operation in every company, and nobody names it. How a decision in her head becomes an action by somebody else. How a piece of information reaches the person who needs it without passing through her first. That one she never built, because she was busy building the first. A body with strong legs and a damaged spine can carry an enormous amount of weight. What it can't do is coordinate itself. Which is why, at eleven at night, it all still comes back to her.

3 · WORK FLOWS (post 6 · ~60s)
In a body, health has less to do with strength than with circulation. When flow is interrupted, the tissue upstream swells and the tissue downstream starves. Most operational pain in a small company is a circulation problem, not a strength problem. Information pools in one person. Decisions back up at the top. Approvals accumulate under the very honourable banner of quality control, until oversight has quietly become congestion. And the instinct, every time, is more control. More checking. The fix is the opposite. Find the blockage. Open it. Let responsibility sit as close as it possibly can to the information. Work should flow through your company. It should not pool in you.

4 · EACH VOLUME, ITS OWN COLOUR (post 13 · ~15s, light, no lesson)
Each volume of the book gets its own colour. This fortnight was silver and bone, because the skeleton is the thing you never think about until it fails. Next is blue. The nervous system. Just in time for Black Friday.

5 · HOLD YOUR FACE (post 15 · ~65s, your own story, so tell it rather than read it)
There was a Tuesday I've never forgotten. I'd spent the whole morning composing myself for a meeting. Difficult news, my own exhaustion, a private fear I'd told nobody. I ran the meeting well. I asked the right questions. I would have told you I'd given nothing away. That evening one of my team came to my desk and said, are you okay? We were all a bit worried about you in there. Every person in that room had spent an hour with a slightly raised heart rate, reading me, unable to give the work their whole attention. A nervous system can't be performed. State leaks through the face, the voice, the eyes, the pace. You can hold it for about an hour. The team reads the rest.

6 · THE DOME (post 19 · ~50s)
In architecture, a dome spreads load across its whole surface, so no single stone carries more than it was made to hold. A regulated leader does that for her team. Pressure arrives, constantly, and she holds it at an altitude where it can be considered rather than felt as raw threat. A cracked dome leaks downward. People start feeling the cash-flow fear they have no visibility into. They work harder, apologise more, speak less. So, one question. What pressures are in my life right now that my team can feel, even though I've never named them?

7 · THE WEEK AFTER (post 26 · ~45s, quiet)
Culture is the ground everyone stands on. Leadership is the signal you're sending across it, right now. And what you transmitted this week, repeated across years, is what settles into the soil. So before Monday, two questions. What did your team learn about you under pressure? And what do you want them to have learned? The gap between those two answers is next fortnight's work, because it's about the relationships that carried the load. Volume X. Symbiosis.

(Post 25, Black Friday, is one line, said to camera or not at all: "To everyone shipping today: this way, and it's survivable.")
