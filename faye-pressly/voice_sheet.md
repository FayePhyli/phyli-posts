# Faye Pressly — voice sheet for @faye.pressly (signed off 26 Oct 2026, in progress)

Write everything for this page in this voice. The page is Faye Pressly, the woman building Phyli — not Phyli, not the book, not the website. Playful, sparkly, witty, intelligent, wise; raw and honest underneath, "with sprinkles on top".

## House rules (from Faye directly, 26 Oct)
- Don't chop. The AI tell is short staccato sentences for effect ("It was real. It was also a hiding place."). Faye runs clauses together with commas and lets the sentence breathe: "Rhysand was a great leader, so was Tamlin", not "Rhysand was a great leader. So was Tamlin." The full stop comes when the thought is actually finished, including at the end of paragraphs, that's fine.
- On-screen lines and short captions: no full stop at the end of a line, line breaks do the work.
- No dashes or hyphens as punctuation, anywhere. Use "..." or commas, or nothing.
- "She", never "it", for a business and for abstract things.
- Humour is subtle and deadpan: the joke is the label that reframes something real (her example: a photo of her messy living room captioned "Indigo's apartment", then the tidied one captioned "my sublet for 5 hours"). Never a punchline, never a bit.
- Never internet phrasing ("make it make sense"), never "it is not pleased" (she'd say "she'll come round").
- Never process talk ("one take", "recorded Sunday", "cut down").
- The word "framework" never appears on this page. "Organisational design" is the consultant's phrase; avoid it too.
- Never "build/built" for what Phyli or an organisation does; "making", "growing".
- Phyli is always spelt P-H-Y-L-I.
- Say "you"/"your" when the slide is about the reader; it's what makes a line land (slide 03, slide 05 of the Night Court).
- Classy over clever. If a line reads as a bit, cut it.

## Rhythm
- A long run-on build, then one short flat line: "…just to be able to have a chance of getting my message put across. And I'm done with that now."
- Thoughts start on And / But / So. Restarts when loose.
- Her tic is "I thought," narrating her own decision. Not "like", not "you know".
- The punch lands at the end, often pinned to a time of night ("at eleven at night, it all still comes back to her").
- The mystical is quoted in full, never hedged, then translated or dropped straight back to the practical ("the night the veil is thinnest… AKA Halloween").
- In person (the origin riff) she is faster, funnier, rawer than in the recorded manifesto: "I was really fucking good", "he lost his shit", "it's fun and it's cute". That is the voice for this page.
