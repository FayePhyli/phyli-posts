1 · THE MANIFESTO, SPOKEN  (3–4 min)  → tonight's reel
=====================================================
Read it to one friend, slowly. Keep the pauses. Don't perform it.

A note, before this page changes.

For almost twenty years I've run and grown the inside of companies, for other people.

I've spent the last two years watching everyone else's stories. From today I'm making mine.

Now I'm building Phyli: a place for my thoughts, wisdom and ideas to come to life, for other women running and growing their own companies as if they are alive.

Because they are.

I want to curate the most beautiful business book in the world. Twelve volumes. Pages that open out, lift and unfold, the way the books we loved as children did. Made by hand, from the materials of couture. Carrying the content of a business book that feels timeless.

Adult books stopped being beautiful a long time ago. Made as cheap as possible, shipped to as many as possible. This is the opposite of that. A book you keep, and hand down.

Because the women building thoughtful companies deserve wisdom that doesn't shout. Because I needed it, and couldn't find it. Because this has felt channelled for a long time, and I'd rather be the hands than argue with it.

And because one day my daughter will ask what I made with my life. I want the answer to be beautiful.

I care about truth, beauty, craft, and work that outlives the week it was posted in.

What I don't care for: hacks, hustle, being everywhere, going viral. I'm not trying to be fast. I'm trying to be aligned, and connected, for a very long time.

My dream is to launch in an art gallery. The Collectors' Volumes, numbered and bound by hand, by masters of their trade. MoMA's bookshop. Vogue. A business book you'd leave on the table because it's beautiful, and then actually read.

If you read it, I want you to pause. To recognise something you already knew. To find pleasure in it. Not another fast thing to consume, and extract from you.

And it will not take over my life. I'll share what I know without draining myself to the bone, because the most important work I'll ever do is being Indigo's mother.

So not everything here will be perfect. It'll be beautifully imperfect. Occasionally cringe. I'm playing with a bigger picture, which mostly terrifies me. And I've decided to stay optimistic, and to keep moving towards something better for her, and for her children.

I've spent most of my life helping other people shine. It was real. It was also a hiding place. Throughout my career, being seen never felt safe.

I give no fucks now.     (beat)     Well. Maybe five percent.

This page is for women building companies with taste and ambition, and for anyone who loves one. If that's you, stay.

And if you're here because you love me: being here is support, and I feel it.

Thank you for watching me do the thing.

3 · VISION FIRST  (3 min)  → Thursday, Volume I reel
=====================================================
Volume One. Vision. Biological lens: the womb. Say it in your words; these are yours from the manuscript, use them freely.

Open with one of these:
- "Begin where everything begins, which is not in a meeting."
- "Almost everyone has set a vision. Almost nobody trusts the results."
- "Vision is not a mental exercise. It's a receptive state."

Then, flowing:
- The room everyone knows: the offsite that produced a beautiful statement nobody could repeat a fortnight later. The strategy day with enormous energy and zero change. The vision you wrote yourself and read back six weeks on, slightly embarrassed, as if you'd been caught performing a version of yourself you didn't believe.
- The open secret: we built an industry on the idea that vision is something you produce. Book the room, bring the snacks, apply enough brainpower, and a vision comes out the other end. Sometimes a document does. But agreement is not conception.
- Where does anything new actually come from? Not in business. In life. Conception needs three things in the same place at the same time: an egg, a sperm, and a womb that can hold what they make.
  · The egg is what's already inside the business: the lived reality, the tension, the intuition, the quietly held desire nobody has found words for. It isn't made in the workshop. It's there before anyone books a room. This is why imported visions fail: a strategy lifted from a competitor or a consultant who flew in on Tuesday arrives with no egg to meet. It floats, impresses briefly, dissolves.
  · The sperm is the catalytic impulse: a founder naming what can no longer continue, a hard line, a question that makes the status quo indefensible. On its own it makes nothing. A leader who brings only urgency and answers, with no receptivity to meet it, doesn't create vision. She creates exhausted people. Most common founder failure, and it's almost always mistaken for leadership.
  · The womb is the container: a system regulated and safe enough to receive something before it's asked to perform.
- Every clear idea you've ever had arrived in the shower, on a walk, in the half-hour before sleep. Never in the third hour of a workshop. Clarity is what becomes available once a system stops bracing. You can't think your way into vision any more than you can will yourself to sleep.
- Vision is sensed before it is named, and known before it can be justified. If you've ever been certain of a direction and unable to explain why for weeks: you were pregnant with it. The language came later.
- Close: why this is Volume One. Before a company, before a strategy, before a single hire, something has to be conceived. Get the conditions right and one conversation changes everything. Get them wrong and you can run offsites until everyone's hoarse.

4 · WHAT I GOT WRONG  (3 min)  → week two
=====================================================
The law-firm story, told straight. It's in the manuscript; tell it as it happened.
- The founder who wanted a vision with an intensity you've rarely seen since. "We need our North Star." The cleared diaries, the booked rooms, the borrowed meeting room at a law firm, small and airless, disappearing under Post-its. Whole days. Leaving tired, hungry and oddly reassured, because a day of talking feels like work.
- Never agreeing. Months of it. Then simply stopping.
- A year later she wrote it. No workshop, no facilitator, no wall of notes. The first version anyone believed, because by then it was simply true and everyone could feel it. The deck just caught it as it landed.
- What you got wrong: you thought vision was produced by effort. What it needed was time, truth, and a container.
- Then, if you want, the personal version: the pressure to know what you were doing with your life, saying "I can feel something forming and I don't know its name yet" to someone frightened, and trying to give it a shape it hadn't earned. Fashion, DJing, back to consultancy. And what it actually was.
